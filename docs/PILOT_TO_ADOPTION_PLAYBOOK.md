# Pilot to Adoption Playbook

This playbook provides a controlled route from an AI opportunity to repeatable organisational use.

## Stage 1: Discovery

**Purpose:** confirm the business problem and whether AI assistance is appropriate.

**Activities:** map the workflow, interview users, identify ownership, assess information readiness, record risks, and define the smallest useful scope.

**Exit evidence:** approved problem statement, process owner, initial risk view, candidate measures, and a proceed, refine, hold, or stop decision.

## Stage 2: Pilot Design

**Purpose:** make the test bounded, observable, and reversible.

**Activities:** name pilot users and sources, define human review, document prohibited actions, establish access, set support and stop conditions, agree evaluation samples, and estimate cost.

**Exit evidence:** pilot brief, approved access, test plan, user guidance, support owner, and rollback or disable path.

## Stage 3: Controlled Pilot

**Purpose:** test usefulness and controls in a realistic but limited setting.

**Activities:** onboard users, observe normal and exception cases, capture corrections and escalations, monitor service health and cost, and hold regular review sessions.

**Exit evidence:** evaluation record, user feedback, incident record, cost observation, known limitations, and an evidence-based recommendation.

## Stage 4: Human Review and Decision

**Purpose:** decide whether the workflow is ready to improve, expand, or stop.

Review:

- output usefulness, accuracy, and source coverage;
- user behaviour, confidence, and training needs;
- access, privacy, security, and policy exceptions;
- operational reliability, support effort, and failure modes;
- cost relative to the value of the completed workflow.

Possible decisions are **scale**, **refine and retest**, **hold**, or **retire**.

## Stage 5: Adoption Preparation

**Purpose:** turn a successful pilot into a supportable service.

**Activities:** assign service ownership, complete documentation, prepare role-based training, establish monitoring and incident response, confirm continuity arrangements, communicate limitations, and approve the production change.

**Exit evidence:** operating model, runbook, training material, support pathway, monitoring plan, approved change, and adoption communications.

## Stage 6: Controlled Scale-Up

**Purpose:** expand without losing visibility or accountability.

Increase users, information, integrations, or permissions in separate increments. Verify each increment before opening the next. Maintain a clear disable path and preserve human authority over material decisions.

## Stage 7: Continuous Improvement

Review quality, adoption, incidents, freshness, cost, and user feedback on an agreed schedule. Retest after material changes to models, information sources, workflow, access, or policy. Retire features that are unused, unsafe, unsupported, or no longer valuable.
